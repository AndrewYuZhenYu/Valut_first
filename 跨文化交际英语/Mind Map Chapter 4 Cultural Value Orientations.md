# Mind Map: Chapter 4 Cultural Value Orientations

## 1. Core Concept: Cultural Patterns
*   **Definition**: Also called value orientation, a term used to collectively discuss values, beliefs, other orientations that characterize the dominant group within a culture.
    *   *Example*: The dominant group in the US: American Anglo-Saxon and Catholic whites.
*   **Components of Cultural Patterns**:
    *   Thinking Model
    *   Values
    *   Norms
    *   Social Practices
*   **Thinking Model (Major Differences)**:
    *   **Westerner**: Rational Thinking (理性思维), Analytical Thinking (分析思维), Objective Thinking (客观性思维), Linear Thinking (线性思维).
    *   **Chinese**: Intuitive Thinking (感性思维), Holistic Thinking (整体思维), Subjective Thinking (主观性思维), Curvilinear Thinking (螺旋型思维).
    *   **Text Discourse**: Oriental thinking mode is circular or curvilinear; native English speakers is linear. Chinese sentences are usually of end weight and the sequence of time and place is arranged from the general to the specific. Chinese discourse development is spiral, starts with a macro entry point, uses a roundabout way to elaborate, spirals and repeats, and gradually narrows the scope.

## 2. Values (The Core of Culture)
*   **Definition**: Value refers to a principle, standard, or quality considered worthwhile or desirable.
*   **Characteristics**:
    *   The core of all the cultures.
    *   A guide to people in a culture.
    *   Applied in every aspect in the life.
    *   It sets norms.
*   **Geert Hofstede's Onion Model**:
    *   **Symbols**: Outer layer.
    *   **Heroes**: Indicate persons: Famous or ordinary, Real or imaginary.
    *   **Rituals**: Middle layer.
    *   **Values**: Core of the Culture. Block the interpretation from the outsiders. Social principles, Goals, standards.
*   **Core Value of Traditional Chinese Culture**: Benevolence (仁). (Benevolence, Righteousness, Manner, Wisdom, Credit).

## 4. Kluckhohn & Strodtbeck’s Value Orientation (5 Orientations)

### 4.1. Human Nature Orientation
*   **Humans are evil but perfectible**:
    *   Traditional Western belief (e.g., Puritans in the US): BASICALLY EVIL.
    *   Christian teaching: Born with original sin; Evil as part of humans' nature; Be saved by God; Perfect one's nature by doing good things.
    *   Western values: Mixture of good and evil; The Bible story of Adam and Eve; Confess the bad you do = the 1st step in becoming good; Right and wrong; Guilt culture.
*   **Humans are a mixture of good and evil**:
    *   Everybody has both good side and bad side.
    *   The distrust of human nature.
    *   American political institutions with "checks and balances" (制衡原则): The legislative branch of government and media journalists should keep an eye on the president, state governors and other government officials to make sure they do not do anything evil.
*   **Humans are good but corruptible**:
    *   Confucius Teaching in China and Asian countries.
    *   People believed to be basically good, Children believed to be pure and innocent. Become corrupted as they grow older and have more contact with society. ("Man on earth, Good at birth. The same nature, Varies on nurture.")
    *   It is the responsibility of those in authority, such as parents, teachers and political leaders, to protect the morality of those under their care and to be models of virtue themselves.
    *   Chinese values: Shame culture. (If a person is not upright or unfilial, society will morally criticize him).

### 4.2. Person-Nature Orientation
*   **Subjugation to Nature**:
    *   Found in India and parts of South America.
    *   Natural disasters may have contributed to their belief that nature is a powerful force beyond the control of individuals.
    *   Cultures holding this orientation believe: Most powerful forces; Beyond control; Not be overcome; Must be accepted.
    *   The best way to deal with nature is to pay respect to it and act humbly in the face of cataclysmic external forces.
*   **Harmony with Nature**:
    *   Associated with East Asians, such as Chinese, Japanese and American Indians.
    *   Main Ideas: To live in harmony with nature in every way possible; Strong desire to be part of nature and not control it.
    *   Examples: Nature affects human affairs and human behavior finds response in Nature (天人感应); The law of Nature and the feelings of humanity are in unison (天理人情); Nature accords with human wishes (天从人愿); Nature is angry and people are resentful (天怒人怨); Nature's will brings about human affinity (天意人愿); Nature and humankind turn to one (天与人归).
*   **Mastery Over Nature**:
    *   Characteristic of the Western approach, long tradition of valuing technology, change, and science.
    *   People see a clear separation between humans and nature.
    *   Traditional Western view stems from the Bible: God Creation & Adam, granting power of conquering and controlling to men. "To conquer and direct the forces of nature to our advantage".
    *   Examples: 1492 Christopher Columbus found the new continent (Tame and conquer); Industrial Revolution (1760-1830); The Fourth Industrial Revolution Klaus Schwab.
    *   Issues of Mastery over Nature: Environment pollution and Humans living; Resource exploitation and ecology; Technology innovation and Morality; Species diversity and Earth development.
    *   Consequently, human beings should: Increase awareness of ecology; Grow interest in environment protection; Realize the importance of the harmonious relationship with nature.

### 4.3. Time Orientation
*   **Past-oriented Culture**:
    *   History, established religions, and tradition are extremely important. Respect for the past is especially evident in most Asian nations. Great Britain, France and Belgium also tend to reverence the heritage, the history and tradition.
    *   Chinese value: Tradition is important; Feel more secure when something new is defined as similar to something that occurred in the past; Experience time as a cycle; Time is cyclical.
*   **Present-oriented Culture**:
    *   Hold that the immediate moment carries the most significance. The future is seen as ambiguous, and in a sense, beyond the control of the individual.
    *   Countries like Filipinos, Spain and Latin Americans, enjoy living in the moment. These cultures tend to be more impulsive and spontaneous and often have a casual, relaxed lifestyle.
*   **Future-oriented Culture**:
    *   Value the future. Taking chances, a stress on youth, and optimism are all hallmarks. American culture is a typical future-oriented culture, which emphasizes short-term.
    *   Western value: A strong belief in progress; Look to the future and make plans and set goals; Why doing sth. new: to overcome the limitations of the past or to surpass the accomplishments of the past; Future can be controlled; Time is linear. T is money.
    *   (Note: More and more Chinese are becoming future-oriented.)

### 4.4. Activity Orientation
*   **Doing Orientation (做)**:
    *   Stresses activity and action.
    *   Reflects a culture where the focus is on activity that is often measurable in external accomplishments. Characteristic of U.S. culture (e.g., fast food).
    *   Mottos: JUST DO IT. Impossible is nothing!
    *   Western values: Talk a lot (planning, problem-solving, gathering information or making decisions); Mental activities lead to action or are the equivalent of action; What do you do: "I do" (e.g., I design software); A sense of urgency (deadline, schedule).
*   **Being Orientation (存在)**:
    *   Characteristics: To accept people, events, and ideas as flowing spontaneously; To stress release, indulgence of existing desires, and working for the moment.
    *   Examples: Most Latino current activity matters the most; Mexicans value the interpersonal relations than accomplishments, take great delight in the conversation with families and friends.
    *   Chinese values: Behave in ways appropriate to their positions (status, social roles, and character) in life; Social status and position are more significant than what the person does; What do you do: "I am" (e.g., I am a manager).
*   **Being-in-Becoming Orientation (成为)**:
    *   Stressing the idea of development and growth.
    *   Emphasizing the kind of activity contributing to one's own overall development; Valuing a spiritual life over a material one.
    *   Example: Hinduism and Buddhism. There is a goal, but it is not measurable (as are awards or status or money), but is instead internal. It is the development of the whole person, such as is achieved through some forms of Eastern meditation.

### 4.5. Relational Orientation
*   **Hierarchy Orientation**:
    *   Chinese values: The society as a whole consists of a series of hierarchies. A person who is at the top of one hierarchy may be at the bottom of another hierarchy that is above it. The criteria may be based on age, family social position, academic achievement, etc. (e.g., Caring the elder people in the family).
    *   Characteristics: Obey authority; Know your place; Treat others according to their position; Look to leading figures to know what to think and do.
*   **Group Orientation**:
    *   Characteristics: Respond to what others think and feel; Depend on others and let them depend on you; be loyal; Look to others in your group to know what to think and do.
*   **Individualism Orientation**:
    *   Characteristics: Express your own feelings and ideas; Be as independent and self-reliant as possible; Make your own decisions and choices.

## 5. Summary Table (Kluckhohn & Strodtbeck Orientations)
*   **Human Nature**: Basically Good (Native Am., Hindu); Mixture of Good and Evil (Europeans); Basically Evil (Puritans, Christianity).
*   **Relationship of Man to Nature**: Nature controls Humans (India, South Am. Native Am.); Harmony With Nature (East Asia: China, Japan, Thailand, Native Am.); Humans controls Nature (North Am.).
*   **Sense of Time**: Past-oriented (China, Japan, UK, France); Present-oriented (Philippines, Latin Am., African Am.); Future-oriented (US dominant cul.).
*   **Activity**: Being (Latino); Growing (Hinduism, Buddhism); Doing (Dominant Am. Culture).
*   **Social Relationship**: Hierarchy (China); Group (Native Am.); Individual (US).

## 6. Limitations of Kluckhohn's Model
*   Not everyone in a culture has the same basic values.
*   Cultures change over time.
*   The concept of basic values is itself a generalization.
*   Within any culture, a preferred set of solutions will be chosen by most people. However, not all people from a culture will make exactly the same set of choices, and, in fact, some people from each culture will select other alternatives.
